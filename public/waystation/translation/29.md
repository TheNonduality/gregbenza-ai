# Entry 29 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "Attention" (*manaskāra*) defined

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: LOCKED

## Sanskrit

*manaskāraḥ katamaḥ | cetasa ābhogaḥ | ālambana-citta-dhāraṇa-karmakaḥ ||*

## English

What counts as *attention* (*manaskāra*)? *Attention* is the turning of the mind toward something. Its function is to hold the mind on the object.

## Note

*Ābhoga* literally means bending or curving toward — the orienting move where the mind turns to face an object before settling on it. "Turning toward" lands plainer than "bending" in English. Xuanzang's 發動 (rousing/activating) takes a slightly different angle — energetic onset rather than directional turn — but the function clause locks the meaning either way.

*Manaskāra* as "attention" is conventional Buddhist English. The risk: in modern usage "attention" can read passive (something one has) where Asaṅga's term is active (the turning move itself). Held for now; revisits if the passive reading creates trouble downstream.
