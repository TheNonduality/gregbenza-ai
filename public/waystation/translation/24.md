# Entry 24 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "How the feeling-aggregate breaks down" (opening)

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: LOCKED

## Sanskrit

*vedanā-skandha-vyavasthānaṃ katamat | ṣaḍ vedanā-kāyāḥ | cakṣuḥ-saṃsparśajā vedanā śrotra-ghrāṇa-jihvā-kāya-manaḥ-saṃsparśajā vedanā || evaṃ ṣaḍ vedanā-kāyāḥ sukhā vā duḥkhā aduḥkhāsukhā vā ||*

## English

How does the feeling-aggregate break down? Feeling arises from contact at each of the six senses — eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind. Each of these can be pleasant, unpleasant, or neither.

## Note

*Vyavasthāna* is rendered "break down / break into" across this and parallel entries (14, 16). It's the structural laying-out that classifies an aggregate into its parts or kinds. *Katamat* alone (without *vyavasthāna*) renders as "what counts as" — a different question.

The Sanskrit announces "six *vedanā-kāyas*" before listing them — standard Sanskrit Abhidharma count-then-enumerate. English doesn't need the announcement; the enumeration counts itself. Dropping the count-sentence is a register-fit move, not a Sanskrit-fidelity loss.

*Aduḥkhāsukhā* renders "neither" — matching the *ubhaya-viparīta* lock from Entries 19 and 21. The Sanskrit structure is the same: the third item is logical negation of the first two, not a positive middle category.

The *sukhā/duḥkhā/aduḥkhāsukhā* triad as adjective-with-*vedanā* renders "pleasant / unpleasant / neither" — the hedonic feeling-tone sense. *Duḥkha* in this construction stays small and immediate; the standalone-noun rendering "unsatisfying" (locked Entry 40) would overreach feeling-tone register. Grammatical role fixes scope: adjective-with-*vedanā* = hedonic, bare noun in other contexts = the full structural sense.
