# Entry 41 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "*Non-delusion*" (*amoha*) defined

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: LOCKED

## Sanskrit

*amohaḥ katamaḥ | vipākato vā āgamato vā adhigamato vā jñānaṃ pratisaṃkhyā | duścaritāpravṛtti-saṃniśraya-dāna-karmakaḥ ||*

## English

What counts as *non-delusion* (*amoha*)? *Non-delusion* is the kind of knowing that sorts things out clearly, whether from karmic ripening, from study, or from direct realization. Its function is to prevent misconduct from arising.

## Note

Third wholesome root; closes the *alobha / adveṣa / amoha* triad. The three sources (*vipāka* = karmic ripening, *āgama* = study/scripture, *adhigama* = direct realization/attainment) match Xuanzang's 報教證 exactly.

*Jñānaṃ pratisaṃkhyā* is two near-synonyms in apposition — "knowing, [that is,] careful discernment." English compresses them into one phrase ("the kind of knowing that sorts things out clearly") because the Sanskrit appositional stack reads stiff in plain English.

Function clause same as Entries 39 and 40 — the three wholesome roots share "prevent misconduct from arising" per the §9 saṃniśraya-dāna rule. Sthiramati waves this one off too (covered by his *hryādayaḥ sugamatvānna vibhajyante* skip-comment at the head of the wholesome eleven).
