# Entry 20 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "The sense-object smell (*gandha*) defined"

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: LOCKED

## Sanskrit

*gandhaḥ katamaḥ | catvāri mahā-bhūtāny upādāya ghrāṇendriya-grāhyo 'rthaḥ | yathā surabhir asurabhiḥ sama-gandhaḥ sahaja-gandhaḥ sāṃyogika-gandhaḥ pāriṇāmika-gandhaś ca ||*

## English

What counts as smell? It is what the nose-sense picks up, and is derived from the four great elements. A smell can be fragrant, foul, or neutral. It can be a scent that comes with the thing itself, a scent that comes from things combining, or a scent that comes from things changing.

## Note

The Sanskrit is a flat six-item list under *yathā*. The English splits it into two classifications — quality (fragrant/foul/neutral) and source (intrinsic/combined/transformed) — because the six items naturally divide that way and a flat English list would lose the structure. Each compound (*sahaja-gandha*, *sāṃyogika-gandha*, *pāriṇāmika-gandha*) expands into a relative clause; one Sanskrit word becomes "scent that comes with the thing itself," etc. Sthiramati gives examples (sandalwood, incense burning, ripe fruit) that we did not import — they belong in the bhāṣya, not in Asaṅga's voice.
