# Entry 26 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "How the perception-aggregate breaks down"

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: LOCKED

## Sanskrit

*saṃjñā-skandha-vyavasthānaṃ katamat | ṣaṭ saṃjñā-kāyāḥ | cakṣuḥ-saṃsparśajā saṃjñā śrotra-ghrāṇa-jihvā-kāya-manaḥ-saṃsparśajā saṃjñā | yathā sa-nimittam api saṃjānāti animittam api parīttam api mahad-gatam api apramāṇam api nāsti kiṃcid ity ākiṃcanya-āyatanam api saṃjānāti || sa-nimitta-saṃjñā katamā | avyavahāra-kuśalasya animitta-dhātu-samāpannasya bhavāgra-samāpannasya ca saṃjñāṃ sthāpayitvā yāvad anyā saṃjñā || animitta-saṃjñā katamā | yā sthāpitā saṃjñā || parīttā saṃjñā katamā | yayā kāma-dhātuṃ saṃjānāti || mahad-gatā saṃjñā katamā | yayā rūpa-dhātuṃ saṃjānāti || apramāṇa-saṃjñā katamā | yayā ākāśa-ānantya-āyatanaṃ vijñāna-ānantya-āyatanaṃ saṃjānāti || ākiṃcanya-saṃjñā katamā | yayā ākiṃcanya-āyatanaṃ saṃjānāti ||*

## English

How does the perception-aggregate break down? There are six perception-bodies, one from contact at each sense — eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind. Through these, perception perceives things with features (*sa-nimitta*) and things without features (*animitta*). It perceives the limited (*parītta*), the vast (*mahad-gata*), and the immeasurable (*apramāṇa*). And it perceives the perception "there is nothing whatsoever" — the sphere of nothing-whatsoever (*ākiṃcanya-āyatana*).

What counts as perception of things with features? All perception except in three cases: someone without skill in conventional language, someone absorbed in the featureless-element, and someone at the peak of existence.

What counts as perception of things without features? Exactly those three cases just set aside.

What counts as perception of the limited? Perception that recognizes the desire-realm.

What counts as perception of the vast? Perception that recognizes the form-realm.

What counts as perception of the immeasurable? Perception that recognizes the sphere of infinite space and the sphere of infinite consciousness.

What counts as perception of nothing-whatsoever? Perception that recognizes the sphere of nothing-whatsoever.

## Note

DSBC has *akiñcana saṃjñā* in the sixth definition heading; standard form parallel to *ākiṃcanya-āyatana* would be *ākiṃcanya-saṃjñā*. Silently corrected as OCR slip.

The six categories track meditative cosmology: *sa-nimitta* covers ordinary cognition; *animitta* covers signless absorptions and non-language beings; *parītta* the desire-realm; *mahad-gata* the form-realm; *apramāṇa* the first two formless spheres; *ākiṃcanya* the third formless sphere. Bhavāgra (the fourth formless, "peak of existence") is excluded from *sa-nimitta* via Asaṅga's three-case carve-out but doesn't get its own positive category — it sits inside *animitta*.
