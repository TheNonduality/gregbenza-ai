# Entry 42 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "*Zeal*" (*vīrya*) defined

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: PROVISIONAL

## Sanskrit

*vīryaṃ katamat | kuśale cetaso 'bhyutsāhaḥ sannāhe vā prayoge vā alīnatve vā avyāvṛttau vā asantuṣṭau vā | kuśala-pakṣa-paripūraṇa-pariniṣpādana-karmakam ||*

## English

What counts as *zeal* (*vīrya*)? *Zeal* is the mind exerting itself toward what is wholesome — in armoring up, in engaging the work, in not shrinking back, in not turning away, in not stopping short. Its function is to fulfill and complete what is wholesome.

## Note

The five locatives map a Mahayana sequence: *sannāha* (initial resolve, the armor-stage), *prayoga* (actually engaging the work), *alīna* (not collapsing back when difficulty hits), *avyāvṛtti* (not reversing under setback), *asantuṣṭi* (not stopping at partial gain). Sthiramati treats these as the stages where *vīrya* operates. Sanskrit and Xuanzang match item-by-item — 被甲 / 方便 / 無下 / 無退 / 無足. *Abhyutsāha* is literally "up-rising-out-toward" — vigor with directional lift. Function clause is *karmakam* alone — direct function, not *saṃniśraya-dāna*. Sits with the three wholesome roots in this non-saṃniśraya pattern.

## Open question

*Vīrya* rendered as "zeal" provisionally. The category is wholesome by construction — not direction-neutral. *Vīrya* names this charge only when directed toward the wholesome; if directed at the unwholesome it would be a kleśa (*rāga*, *krodha*). The English needs to carry that built-in wholesome direction along with the lift/charge. "Energy" tested and rejected (too inert — describes a battery). "Wholeheartedness" tested — carried both built-wholesome and charge, but didn't capture the zealous-enthusiastic feeling. "Zeal" carries the charge and directedness but doesn't carry the built-in wholesomeness. Revisit when *vīrya* fires in technical clusters downstream — seven bodhyaṅgas, four right exertions, noble eightfold path.
