# Entry 19 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "The sense-object sound (*śabda*) defined"

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: LOCKED

## Sanskrit

*śabdaḥ katamaḥ | catvāri mahā-bhūtāny upādāya śrotra-indriya-grāhyo 'rthaḥ | manojño vā amanojño vā ubhaya-viparīto vā | upātta-mahā-bhūta-hetuko vā anupātta-mahā-bhūta-hetuko vā tad-ubhayo vā | loka-prasiddho vā siddhopanīto vā parikalpito vā āryair-deśito vā tīrthair-deśito vā ||*

## English

What counts as sound? It is what the ear-sense picks up, and is derived from the four great elements. It can be pleasing, displeasing, or neither. It can come from a living body, from external matter, or from both. It can be everyday speech, teaching reported from someone's direct realization, or teaching built from someone's constructed conceptual system. It can be teaching from the noble ones, or teaching from outsiders.

## Note

The five speech-items split into two classifications under Sthiramati's reading: three teaching-varieties (*loka-prasiddha* / *siddhopanīta* / *parikalpita*) followed by two source-varieties (*ārya-deśita* / *tīrtha-deśita*). The English structure preserves this split.

*Parikalpita* in this list is the doctrinal-construction sense: speech whose content is built up from conceptual elaboration rather than reported from direct realization. "Teaching built from someone's constructed conceptual system" lands the source-distinction in plain English. Sthiramati glosses *parikalpita* as *tīrthya-deśita* (taught by non-Buddhist teachers); we declined to import that gloss into Asaṅga's voice.

*Ārya-deśita / tīrtha-deśita* renders "teaching from the noble ones, or teaching from outsiders." Earlier draft had "spoken truthfully or untruthfully" — that imported Sthiramati's downstream classification of *tīrtha* speech as false, while Asaṅga's pair is a source-distinction (who is teaching), not a truth-distinction (whether it's true). Caught in cross-review with ChatGPT.

The *manojña/amanojña/ubhaya-viparīta* triad locks here as "pleasing, displeasing, or neither." "Neither" preserves *ubhaya-viparīta*'s logical structure (negation of both prior categories) where "neutral" would have posited a middle category the Sanskrit doesn't claim. Matches Entry 21.

The *grāhya / gocara* distinction: form (Entry 18) uses *gocara* (range/field); sound, smell, taste, and touch (Entries 19–22) all use *grāhya* (what gets grabbed). Pattern confirmed across the four — form alone is the *gocara* outlier.
