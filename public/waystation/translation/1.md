# Entry 1 — Chapter 1, Section 1, opening

## Sanskrit

*namo buddhāya ||*

*[atha mūlavastuni tridharmaparicchedaḥ prathamaḥ |]*

*tridharmaḥ saṃgrahaḥ saṃprayogo 'nvayaś ca lakṣaṇe |*
*viniścaye satyadharmau prāptiḥ sāṃkathyam eva ca ||*

*kati kasmād upādānaṃ vyavasthānaṃ ca lakṣaṇam |*
*anukram·ārtha·dṛṣṭānta·bhedā jñeyāḥ samuccaye ||*

*skandhā dhātava āyatanāni ca kati | skandhāḥ pañca | rūpaskandho vedanāskandhaḥ saṃjñāskandhaḥ saṃskāraskandho vijñānaskandhaś ca || dhātavo 'ṣṭādaśa | cakṣur·dhātuḥ rūpa·dhātuś cakṣur·vijñāna·dhātuḥ, śrotra·dhātuḥ śabda·dhātuḥ śrotra·vijñāna·dhātuḥ, ghrāṇa·dhātur gandha·dhātur ghrāṇa·vijñāna·dhātuḥ, jihvā·dhātuḥ rasa·dhātur jihvā·vijñāna·dhātuḥ, kāya·dhātuḥ spraṣṭavya·dhātuḥ kāya·vijñāna·dhātuḥ, mano·dhātur dharma·dhātur mano·vijñāna·dhātuś ca || āyatanāni dvādaśa | cakṣur·āyatanaṃ rūpāyatanaṃ śrotrāyatanaṃ śabd·āyatanaṃ ghrāṇāyatanaṃ gandh·āyatanaṃ jihv·āyatanaṃ ras·āyatanaṃ kāy·āyatanaṃ spraṣṭavyāyatanaṃ mana·āyatanaṃ dharmāyatanaṃ ca ||*

## English

Homage to the Buddha.

[Now, in the root matter, the first chapter: on the three factors.]

This work has two parts.

The first part — the Compendium of Hallmarks — has four sections: *the three factors*, *grouping*, *conjunction*, and *accompaniment*.

The second part — the Compendium of Determining — also has four sections: *the truths*, *the teaching*, *attainment*, and *dialectic*.

Every topic in this work gets the same set of questions. How many are there? Why that number? What do they include? How are they arranged? What is their defining hallmark? What is their order? What do they mean? What are some illustrations? And what are the subtypes? Keep these in mind as you read.

How many aggregates, elements, and gateways are there?

There are five aggregates. The form-aggregate (*rūpa-skandha*), the feeling-aggregate (*vedanā-skandha*), the perception-aggregate (*saṃjñā-skandha*), the formation-aggregate (*saṃskāra-skandha*), and the consciousness-aggregate (*vijñāna-skandha*).

There are eighteen elements: six pairs of sense and object, plus the six consciousnesses that arise from them. The eye and form, with eye-consciousness. The ear and sound, with ear-consciousness. The nose and smell, with nose-consciousness. The tongue and taste, with tongue-consciousness. The body and touch, with body-consciousness. And the mind and mental object, with mind-consciousness.

Then there are twelve gateways — the same six pairs of sense and object, without the consciousnesses. Eye and form. Ear and sound. Nose and smell. Tongue and taste. Body and touch. Mind and mental object.
