# Entry 27 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "How the formation-aggregate breaks down" (opening)

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: LOCKED

## Sanskrit

*saṃskāra-skandha-vyavasthānaṃ katamat | ṣaṭ cetanā-kāyāḥ | cakṣuḥ-saṃsparśajā cetanā śrotra-ghrāṇa-jihvā-kāya-manaḥ-saṃsparśajā cetanā yayā kuśalatvāya cetayate saṃkleśāya cetayate avasthābhedāya cetayate itīyaṃ cetanā vedanāṃ saṃjñāṃ ca sthāpayitvā tadanye caitasikā dharmāś cittaviprayuktāś ca saṃskārāḥ saṃskāra-skandha ityucyate ||*

## English

How does the formation-aggregate break down? It breaks down into six intentions. One for each sense-contact: eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind. This intention directs the mind toward what's wholesome, toward what's defiled, or toward neutral activity. The formation-aggregate includes this intention, formations not bound to mind, plus all the other mental factors except feeling and perception.

## Note

The triad here is *kuśalatva / saṃkleśa / avasthābheda*, not the *kuśala / akuśala / avyākṛta* triad we locked in Entry 8 for saṃskāra's hallmark. Wholesome / defiled / state-distinction rather than the strict ethical categories. The English asymmetry ("wholesome" vs "defiled") mirrors the Sanskrit — *kuśalatva* and *saṃkleśa* are not a clean adjective pair. *Cetayate* renders as "directs the mind toward." Entry 8's *prerayati* lock as "drives the mind" stays separate — two distinct Sanskrit verbs, two distinct English verbs.
