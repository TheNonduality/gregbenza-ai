# Entry 21 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "The sense-object taste (*rasa*) defined"

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: LOCKED

## Sanskrit

*rasaḥ katamaḥ | catvāri mahā-bhūtāny upādāya jihvendriya-grāhyo 'rthaḥ | tikto 'mlo madhuraḥ kaṭuko lavaṇaḥ kaṣāyaś ca | manojño vā amanojño vā ubhaya-viparīto vā sahajo vā sāṃyogiko vā pāriṇāmiko vā ||*

## English

What counts as taste? It is what the tongue-sense picks up, and is derived from the four great elements. Bitter, sour, sweet, spicy, salty, and astringent. A taste can be pleasing, displeasing, or neither. It can be a flavor that comes with the thing itself, a flavor that comes from things combining, or a flavor that comes from things changing.

## Note

Xuanzang T.1605 has 淡 (bland) for the sixth rasa where DSBC has *kaṣāya* (astringent). The standard Indian six-rasa list (Ayurveda and Abhidharma both) has *kaṣāya* — Xuanzang's 淡 is unusual; he doesn't use 澀, the standard Chinese for astringent. Possible variant in his source manuscript. The English follows the Sanskrit.

The *manojña/amanojña/ubhaya-viparīta* triad locks here as "pleasing, displeasing, or neither" — same as Entry 19. "Neither" preserves *ubhaya-viparīta*'s logical structure (negation of both prior categories) where "neutral" would have posited a middle category the Sanskrit doesn't claim.
