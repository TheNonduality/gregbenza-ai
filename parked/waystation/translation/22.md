# Entry 22 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "The sense-object touch (derived portion) defined"

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: LOCKED

## Sanskrit

*spraṣṭavyaikadeśaḥ katamaḥ | catvāri mahā-bhūtāny upādāya kāyendriya-grāhyo 'rthaḥ | ślakṣṇatvaṃ karkaśatvaṃ laghutvaṃ gurutvaṃ picchilatvaṃ mandatvam amandatvaṃ śītatvam uṣṇatvaṃ jighatsā pipāsā tṛptir balaṃ daurbalyaṃ mūrcchā kaṇḍūtiḥ pūtir vyādhir jarā maraṇaṃ klāntir viśrāma ūrjā ca ||*

## English

What counts as touch? It is what the body-sense picks up, including the four great elements as well as smoothness, roughness, lightness, heaviness, slipperiness, softness, firmness, coldness, heat, hunger, thirst, satiation, strength, weakness, fainting, itching, decay, sickness, aging, dying, exhaustion, rest, and vigor.

## Note

The Sanskrit asks for *spraṣṭavyaikadeśaḥ* — the *ekadeśa* / one-part of touch — not just *spraṣṭavyam*. The four great elements are themselves tactile, and were already listed under "great elements." Form, sound, smell, and taste have no overlap with the great elements, so they get no *ekadeśa* qualifier and the formula "derived from the four great elements" applies cleanly. Touch is structurally different: the great elements ARE part of touch. The English carries this with "including the four great elements as well as..." — keeping the question-form parallel to Entries 18–21 while letting the verb shift do the structural work.

Xuanzang's list (T.1605) has 22 items; Sanskrit has 23. Missing in Chinese is *uṣṇa* (heat) — 冷 appears without parallel 熱. Possible variant in his source. Going with the Sanskrit.

## Open question
