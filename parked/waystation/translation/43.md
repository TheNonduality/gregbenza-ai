# Entry 43 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "*Ease*" (*praśrabdhi*) defined

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: LOCKED

## Sanskrit

*praśrabdhiḥ katamā | kāya-citta-dauṣṭhulyānāṃ pratipraśrabdheḥ kāya-citta-karmaṇyatā | sarvāvaraṇa-niṣkarṣaṇa-karmikā ||*

## English

What counts as *ease* (*praśrabdhi*)? *Ease* is the workability of body and mind that arises when their heaviness subsides. Its function is to dispel all obstructions.

## Note

DSBC has *aśrabdhiḥ* in the opening — OCR slip for *praśrabdhiḥ*. The function clause itself uses *pratipraśrabdheḥ* (same root), and Xuanzang's 安 corresponds to *praśrabdhi*. Silently corrected.

*Praśrabdhi* carries technical *śamatha* weight — marker of meditative workability at the threshold of access concentration. The parenthetical Sanskrit carries that for readers who want it; the function clause (dispelling all obstructions) keeps the English from going soft. Function is *karmikā* alone — direct, not *saṃniśraya-dāna*. *Karmaṇyatā* (workability, fit-for-work) is the structural noun *ease* names. The factor is the workable-state, not the calming-event — per the standing-factor discipline.

## Open question

*Dauṣṭhulya* renders as "heaviness." Xuanzang's 麁重 ("coarse-heavy") supports the somatic-mental drag. But *dauṣṭhulya* is load-bearing across the system — it's the unworkable karmic residue that *āśraya-parāvṛtti* targets, and the English needs to hold that technical weight when it fires in non-*praśrabdhi* contexts. "Difficulty" tested and rejected (flattens to generic obstacle, loses the somatic-load specificity). Revisit when *dauṣṭhulya* fires structurally downstream.
