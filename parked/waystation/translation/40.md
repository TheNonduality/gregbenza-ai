# Entry 40 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "*Non-hatred*" (*adveṣa*) defined

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: LOCKED

## Sanskrit

*adveṣaḥ katamaḥ | sattveṣu duḥkhe duḥkha-sthānīyeṣu ca dharmeṣv anāghātaḥ | duścaritāpravṛtti-saṃniśraya-dāna-karmakaḥ ||*

## English

What counts as *non-hatred* (*adveṣa*)? *Non-hatred* is the mind not lashing out, whether at other beings, at the unsatisfying, or at what brings on the unsatisfying. Its function is to prevent misconduct from arising.

## Note

The three locatives — *sattveṣu* (at sentient beings), *duḥkhe* (at the unsatisfying), *duḥkha-sthānīyeṣu dharmeṣu* (at what brings on the unsatisfying) — mark the three occasions where the lashing-out reflex (*āghāta*) would otherwise fire. Not three objects of attitude. Asaṅga uses the same three locatives later to define *pratigha* (anger — the unwholesome opposite), confirming the locatives as triggers-for-strike-out, not things-to-feel-good-about. English grammar matters here: "non-hatred toward suffering" reads as "you should not hate suffering," importing an attitude-toward-suffering reading the Sanskrit doesn't carry. The locked English ("the mind not lashing out, whether at... or at...") names the absence-of-strike-out without that import.

This entry produced the *duḥkha* lock for the whole project. *Duḥkha* renders here as "the unsatisfying" — the bare-noun rendering for *duḥkha* outside the *vedanā* adjective form. See Glossary. *Duḥkha-sthānīya-dharma* renders "what brings on the unsatisfying" — the source-direction (things that produce *duḥkha*, not what *duḥkha* produces). Xuanzang's 苦具 ("instruments of suffering") confirms the source-reading.

Sthiramati waves off the wholesome eleven entirely: *hryādayaḥ sugamatvānna vibhajyante* — "*hrī* and the rest are not analyzed because they're easy to understand." Doctrinal content for *adveṣa* recovered from his commentary downstream (especially the *pratigha* and *āghāta* glosses, which name *āghāta* as the mental state of a mind already striking out).

Function clause: same machinery as Entries 39 and 41; collapses in English to "prevent misconduct from arising" per the §9 saṃniśraya-dāna rule.
