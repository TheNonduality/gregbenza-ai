# Entry 45 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "*Equanimity*" (*upekṣā*) defined

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: LOCKED

## Sanskrit

*upekṣā katamā | sa-vīryakān alobha-adveṣa-amohān niśritya yā saṃkliṣṭa-vihāra-vairodhikī citta-samatā citta-praśaṭhatā cittasya anābhoga-avasthitatā | saṃkleśa-anavakāśa-saṃniśraya-dāna-karmikā ||*

## English

What counts as *equanimity* (*upekṣā*)? *Equanimity* — resting on *non-greed*, *non-hatred*, *non-delusion*, together with *zeal* — is what counters a defiled mind: first the mind grows even, then it settles on its own, then it rests without effort. Its function is to keep affliction from finding an opening.

## Note

The three-stage gloss — *citta-samatā* (mind grows even) → *citta-praśaṭhatā* (settles on its own) → *cittasya anābhoga-avasthitatā* (rests without effort) — reads as progressive stages of *upekṣā* in *śamatha*. Xuanzang's 心平等性 / 心正直性 / 心無功用住性 presents the same three in the same order. *Anābhoga* recalls *ābhoga* from Entry 29 (*manaskāra* as the active turning of mind toward an object); here, the third stage is the mind no longer needing the attention-turn move. Same root, opposite valence. *Praśaṭhatā* reads from *pra-śratha* (well-loosened, naturally relaxed) rather than *śāṭhya* (cunning); Xuanzang's 正直性 (uprightness) is a different angle on the same middle-stage release.

Same opening as Entry 44 (*apramāda*): both rest on the three wholesome roots + *vīrya*. Asaṅga structurally pairs *apramāda* and *upekṣā* as the wholesome eleven's meta-factors. Function clause *saṃkleśa-anavakāśa-saṃniśraya-dāna-karmikā* collapses per §9 to "keep affliction from finding an opening" — same pattern as the three wholesome roots' *duścaritāpravṛtti* clause. Xuanzang renders *upekṣā* as 捨 (letting-go) — emphasis on the release rather than the resulting evenness; both angles are true.
