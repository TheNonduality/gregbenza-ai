# Entry 4 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "Why 'aggregates with grasping'"

Pradhan page: ~1 · Status: LOCKED

## Sanskrit

*kasmāt skandhā upādānam ity ucyante | upādānena sahitatvāt skandhā upādānam ity ucyante | upādānaṃ katamat | skandheṣu chando rāgaś ca | kasmāc chando rāgaś copādānam ity ucyate | anāgata-vartamāna-skandhānām abhinirvartanato 'parihārataś ca | anāgate 'bhilāṣād vartamāne 'dhyavasānāc ca chando rāgaś copādānam ity ucyate | kasmād dhātava āyatanāni ca sopādāna-dharmā ity ucyante | tatra skandhavan nirdeśaḥ ||*

## English

Why are the aggregates sometimes called "aggregates with grasping" (*upādāna-skandhāḥ*)? Because grasping comes with them.

And what counts as grasping? Grasping is the fixation that shows up where the aggregates are. This fixation has two sides. It reaches toward what hasn't yet arrived, and it clings to what is already here. The reaching pulls the next aggregates into being. The clinging won't release the present ones. This is what grasping is.

The same mechanism applies to the elements and gateways — wherever aggregates, elements, or gateways appear, grasping takes hold.
