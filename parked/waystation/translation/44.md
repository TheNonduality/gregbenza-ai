# Entry 44 — Chapter 1, Section 1, "*Diligence*" (*apramāda*) defined

Pradhan page: ~3 · Status: PROVISIONAL

## Sanskrit

*apramādaḥ katamaḥ | sa-vīryakān alobha-adveṣa-amohān niśritya yā kuśalānāṃ dharmāṇāṃ bhāvanā sāsravebhyaś ca dharmebhyaś cittārakṣā | sa ca laukika-lokottara-sampatti-paripūraṇa-pariniṣpādana-karmakaḥ ||*

## English

What counts as *diligence* (*apramāda*)? *Diligence* — resting on *non-greed*, *non-hatred*, *non-delusion*, together with *zeal* — cultivates what is wholesome and guards the mind from contaminating dharmas (*sāsrava-dharma*). Its function is to fulfill and complete mundane (*laukika*) and supramundane (*lokottara*) attainment.

## Note

DSBC has *amramādaḥ* and *dharmāṇāmbhāvanā* — OCR slips. Silently corrected. Structurally a meta-factor: rests on four others (three wholesome roots + *vīrya*) and does two things — cultivates wholesome dharmas, guards mind from contaminated. Parallel construction to *upekṣā* (Entry 45) — Asaṅga structurally pairs *apramāda* and *upekṣā* as the wholesome eleven's meta-factors. Function clause covers both *laukika* and *lokottara sampatti* — the biggest scope in the wholesome eleven so far.

## Open question

*Apramāda* rendered as "diligence" — provisionally. Tied to *vīrya*'s open question: if *vīrya* moves off "zeal," diligence may need to move back to *vīrya*. "Heedfulness" the conventional Buddhist English but archaic in 2026 American. "Vigilance" biases the guarding side over the cultivation side. "Conscientiousness" too administrative.
