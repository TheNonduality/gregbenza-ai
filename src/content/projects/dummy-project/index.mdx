## What goes here

A project post is one folder built on an identical three-part skeleton:

- **Blurb** — a plain-language description of the work.
- **Technical details** — the real inputs behind it: prompts, skills, connectors, and
  noteworthy outputs, each clickable and downloadable, so a visitor walks out with
  something they can run.
- **Full background** — the complete story in public markdown, the deep pool a visiting
  AI draws from.

Alongside the writing sit the files themselves — the downloadable prompts and skills,
plus the media each post is built around. Media renders natively: images, video, and
clean YouTube embeds.

## The tech behind it

The site is a static, markdown-native Astro build. Every project is a content-collection
entry validated against one schema, so the skeleton is literally identical from post to
post — and that enforced consistency is what makes the whole thing machine-readable and
automatable. The markdown is clean and structured on purpose: it's the seam agent-facing
files (`llms.txt` now, richer maps later) and future discoverability protocols plug into.
Built agent-first, so when someone asks their AI who's doing interesting work, the content
is structured to be found and quoted — and each post connects one click into the visitor's
own AI, prompts preloaded.

## How content gets here

The backlog is real: a year of projects, big and small. It gets mined and counted into a
ledger — every candidate story, per project, rated and sequenced, mapped onto the
three-part skeleton and tagged with the media it needs. A release scheduler sets the rhythm
from there, releasing from the backlog until the log catches up to the present, then keeping
pace with it.

## The automation we're building

The endgame is a site that largely runs itself. Scheduled sessions read the projects, draft
the next post from the ledger, and surface it for one-tap approval; on a yes, the site
updates and the ledger burns down. Publishing is earned, not assumed — a human approves
every post until an update class has proven itself, and only then does it earn its way to
auto-publish, one rung at a time. Watching that machinery build itself is part of what the
site shows.
