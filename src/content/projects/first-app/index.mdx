## Not a programmer

I sell things for a living. I don't write software, and before this week
nothing I'd built had ever had a user besides me.

What I had was a chat workflow — [WorkBuddy and the Margin
Magician](/projects/workbuddy-margin-magician/) — and, from months of
[building games with AI](/?p=games), a suspicion that had been growing all
spring: the chat window could produce more than answers. I'd watched it
build game dashboards and whole playable campaigns. The capability was
clearly there. I just hadn't pointed it at work yet.

## The outputs were becoming programs

Looking back, the app announced itself before I noticed. In mid-May I
asked for an order checklist and got back an HTML file — not a list, a
little working page with checkboxes. Late in May, one morning's questions
produced a dozen interactive revenue charts, each one a file I could open,
tap, and share. Somewhere in those weeks the answers had quietly stopped
being text.

Then came the push. The ERP's own team had seen the Margin Magician
protocol, and their reaction wasn't "nice spreadsheet trick" — it was,
roughly: this is good, why isn't it an app on our API?

## The two-button question

On the evening of May 28 I talked it through with an AI on a drive, and
asked the question this post is named after. I genuinely didn't know. I
assumed apps came from some mountain of tooling, accounts, and approvals,
and that people like me stood at the bottom of it.

The answer: for a web app, you make a file, you open it on the phone, you
tap "Add to Home Screen." That's the mountain.

By the end of that evening a prototype existed under a codename:

## One file, whole app

<Media type="image" src="/projects/first-app/v1-first-morning.png" alt="A phone app screen with a soft mauve background: a header reading The App, a status line reading No data loaded, a greeting reading Hi Greg above instructions to upload an ERP line-item CSV with Margin Magician rules and the master customer list already loaded, and a white settings sheet with an Anthropic API key field saved locally only." caption="Version one, the morning after it was born: one HTML file with the Margin Magician rules baked in — running again today for this screenshot." />

Version one was a single HTML file. Inside it: the chat, the Margin
Magician rules as its brain, the master customer list embedded so it knew
every shop and who owned it, and a paperclip for uploading the weekly CSV
export. No server, no build step, no install beyond the home-screen
button. The entire app could be sent to someone as an attachment —
and was.

It also carried a decision that wouldn't survive the week: the AI key
lived in the phone's settings, saved on the device. Fine for a tool
whose only user is you. Not fine for what happened next.

## The week that followed

Within days the file was rebuilt against the ERP's live API — no more
weekly export, always-current numbers — and that required a real server
to hold the keys the phone no longer could. That server's story is [its
own post](/projects/one-worker-backend/).

Then, on June 4, came the piece that changed what the thing *was*: shared
storage — accounts, tasks, a diary of interactions. It stopped being a
calculator you asked and became a place where the team's work lived. It
had started as a tool for two people. The whole team was using it the day
it appeared.

Seven days from "how do you put an app on a phone" to company software.
The rest of this wing is what it grew into.
